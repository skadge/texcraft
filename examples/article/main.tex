\section{Model}
For small angles the restoring force of a pendulum of length $\ell$ is proportional
to its displacement, which gives the equation of motion
\begin{align}
  \ddot\theta + \frac{g}{\ell}\,\theta &= 0, \label{eq:motion}\\
  \theta(t) &= \theta_0 \cos\left(\sqrt{g/\ell}\;t\right). \label{eq:solution}
\end{align}
Equation~\eqref{eq:solution} solves~\eqref{eq:motion} for a pendulum released at rest.
Its period,
\begin{equation}
  T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{\ell}{g}},
  \label{eq:period}
\end{equation}
does not depend on the amplitude, as long as the amplitude stays small.

\section{Measurements}
We timed ten swings for three lengths:
\begin{itemize}
  \item each length was measured from the pivot to the centre of the bob;
  \item each time is the mean of five runs;
  \item the amplitude stayed below ten degrees.
\end{itemize}
