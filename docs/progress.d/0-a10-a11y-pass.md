- A10 (docs/ROADMAP.md): an accessibility pass. `crates/ui-egui/tests/it/keyboard_paths.rs` drives the window by keyboard and reads egui_kittest's accessibility tree: Tab from nothing crosses the top bar, the rail, the side panel, the editor's tabs and toolbar to the text in reading order; F6 leaves the editor (which keeps Tab for indenting) for the PDF pane, and Tab comes back round to the top; F10 and the arrows walk the menu bar and its menus, Escape gives the keyboard back; the rail and History by the arrows; a file's menu by Shift+F10; Search and the symbol palette from their fields; the editor toolbar's menus, the formatted view's figure, the build log (filters, messages, places, files, raw log), the review margin's thread and reply, the PDF pane's toolbar and menus, and the status bar's controls; and every dialog (each Settings tab, Keyboard Shortcuts, About, a prompt, a confirmation, unsaved changes, the gallery, Open from GitLab/GitHub, the commit dialog, the figure dialog, the AI consent dialog) takes the keyboard, keeps Tab inside, goes back with Shift+Tab and returns the keyboard to the control that had it on Escape. Each step checks that focus moved (no trap) and every focusable node has a role and a name starting with its visible label. Fixed from what they found: dialogs that did not take the keyboard (Settings, About, Keyboard Shortcuts) or give it back (all); the Keyboard Shortcuts window, not modal, let Tab reach the window behind and could not open twice (now craft-keymap's body in a modal of ours); menus opened by Enter or Shift+F10 left the keyboard on their button; the PDF pane came before the editor in Tab order, the outline before the files, the PDF toolbar's pages and zoom and the status bar's end right to left, the status bar before the rail, a version's changes before the versions; F6 from inside a region went back to the first region; opening a version's changes, and leaving focus mode by its button, lost the keyboard; three resize handles all named "Resize panel"; folders did not say whether they were open; the build log's places were clickable labels, not links; Settings' sections were tree items, not tabs.
