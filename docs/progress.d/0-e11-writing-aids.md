- E11 (docs/ROADMAP.md): writing aids. **Spelling** comes from the suite's proofing library, `craft-proof` at the pinned craft-libs rev: English always (its built-in Moby word list, public domain; SCOWL's en-US/en-GB/en-CA/en-AU Hunspell dictionaries when the build sets `CRAFT_I18N_DIR`), other languages from Hunspell dictionaries or language packs installed on the desktop; a language with none is reported ("There is no spelling dictionary for fr: install the Hunspell dictionary fr_FR…") and never checked against another. `texcraft-latex`'s `prose::ranges` gives the text a reader reads, from the tolerant tree: commands and their keys and options (`\ref`, `\label`, `\cite`, `\usepackage`, `\setlength`, `\texttt`…), environment names and arguments, math, verbatim, `minted`, `lstlisting` and drawing environments are skipped, comments too unless asked for, the preamble except the title block, and a word touching an accent command (`caf\'e`); `prose::document_language` reads babel's options (its `main=`, the last language, also from the class options) and polyglossia's `\setdefaultlanguage`. The engine finds a file's words again only when its text changes and keeps each word's verdict, so the editor checks the lines in view (and 40 around) every frame at the cost of a lookup per new word; suggestions are asked for only when a word's menu or popup opens. The user's words (kept with the preferences), the project's `.texcraft/words.txt` (in its folder; for the session without one) and words ignored for the session count as right. In the source, misspelled words get a wavy underline in the new `spelling` theme token; resting on one, or Quick Fix, shows its spellings as fixes; the text's context menu (right-click, Shift+F10) starts with the spellings, Add to Dictionary and Ignore; F7 (Next Misspelling) selects the next one. The formatted view underlines them too. Settings › Editor: Check spelling, the spelling language (from the document, else the interface's; or chosen), with what is checked now or why nothing is, and Check comments. Commands: `language.spelling {path, line?, end_line?, suggestions?}` (misspellings with lines, columns and suggestions, or why nothing is checked), `language.spelling_options {on?, language?, comments?}`, `language.add_word {word, to: user|project}`, `language.ignore_word {word}`; interface commands `edit.spelling.replace {text?}`, `edit.spelling.add {to?}`, `edit.spelling.ignore`, `edit.spelling.next`. **Symbol palette**: `edit.symbols` (Insert › Symbols…, and an Ω button beside Σ in the editor's toolbar) opens a side panel section of our own groups (Greek, relations, operators, arrows, accents, delimiters, miscellaneous, and the recent ones first), each symbol a button drawn as its Unicode glyph and named by its name and command, searchable by either (Enter inserts the first match). A symbol goes in as its command inside a formula and in `$…$` outside one (the caret left inside), an accent's braces around the selection, and one from amssymb or amsmath not loaded by the document says which `\usepackage` it needs; `edit.insert_symbol {command}` does it for agents. Tests: prose extraction (keys, math, verbatim, environments, comments, accents, preamble, hostile input) and the document language (latex); a fake dictionary for marks, places and suggestions, user, project and ignored words, the project list in its folder, no-dictionary and off messages, and the real English dictionary (engine); a spelling from the context menu replacing the word, and the palette inserting `\alpha` in `$…$` then `\beta` inside it (kittest). Not yet: the formatted view has the underline but not the context menu, glyphs the installed fonts lack show as boxes (typeset images from the formulas service are not used for the palette), and dictionaries other than English are not available in the browser.
