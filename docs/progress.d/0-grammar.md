- 2026-11-25: E12 (docs/ROADMAP.md): grammar. **Offline, English**: Harper (`harper-core` 3.2, Apache-2.0, its thesaurus left out) checks the prose of LaTeX files. `texcraft-latex`'s new `passage::passages` joins what `prose::ranges` keeps into the paragraphs, headings, captions and footnotes a reader reads (the sentence around a footnote goes on after it; a blank line, `\par`, `\item`, `\\`, `&` and a non-math environment end a passage): a formula, reference, citation or quantity reads as the placeholder word `X`, an escaped character as itself, a word with accent commands as its letters, ``` `` ```, `''`, `--`, `---` and `~` as typeset; each passage maps its characters back to source bytes and says whether they are written there exactly. Harper's findings are mapped back to the source; one touching a placeholder is dropped (math is not prose), and its replacements are offered only where the source holds the flagged words exactly as written, so taking one keeps the LaTeX. Harper's spelling rule is off and its spelling findings dropped: spelling stays with the spell checker. Harper takes about 10 ms a paragraph and most of a second to load its rules, so on the desktop it runs on its own thread, four passages out at a time, those nearest the lines in view first, once typing has paused for half a second; in the browser, one passage per frame. A passage's findings are kept by its text, so an edit rechecks only the passage it changed; the dialect follows the document's language (en-GB, en-AU, en-CA, en-IN, else American), and a document in another language is not checked offline, saying so. **Display**: grammar problems are underlined with a double straight line in the new `grammar` theme token (teal), apart from spelling's wavy line and hints' dots, in the source and in the formatted view; resting on one, or Quick Fix, shows Harper's message and its replacements as fixes; the text's context menu on one starts with its message, its replacements, Ignore (until TeXCraft closes) and Turn Off This Rule. F7 (now Next Spelling or Grammar Problem) goes through both. Settings › Editor: Check grammar (English), on by default, with what is checked or why nothing is, a switch per group of rules (agreement; grammar and word order; punctuation and capitals; repeated and redundant words; word choice and usage; style and readability, off by default as technical writing trips it on every long sentence), and the rules turned off from the menu, each with Turn On. **With AI, any language**: a new kind of request, Check grammar (`ai.grammar {path, start, end}`; Edit › Check Grammar with AI and the text's context menu, on the selection or the paragraph at the caret), with its own switch in Settings › AI and an editable prompt (placeholders `{passage}`, `{language}`); the model answers `QUOTE:`/`PROBLEM:`/`REPLACEMENT:`, each problem becomes a grammar problem on the first place its quote is in the passage ("Found by AI (model)"), its replacement offered only when it keeps the quote's LaTeX, and a problem whose quote is not there is dropped and counted. Desktop only, as the other AI requests. Commands: `grammar.check {path}` (every problem with lines, columns, rule, group, message, replacements, id and origin, after the checker is done), `grammar.fix {path, id, replacement?}`, `grammar.ignore {id}`, `grammar.rule {rule|group, on}`, `grammar.options {on?}`; interface commands `edit.grammar.replace {text?}`, `edit.grammar.ignore`, `edit.grammar.rule_off`, `edit.ai_grammar`. Tests: passages (commands dropped and math as a word, breaks, headings and footnotes, placeholders, typeset quotes and accents, exact and inexact spans, hostile input) in latex; Harper's findings on the source words with keys and math skipped, no spelling findings, a replacement taken keeping `\textbf{…}` and `\ref{…}`, ignore, a rule and a group off and on, French and off saying why, the editor's call never waiting and the findings arriving afterwards, a 50-page fixture (400 paragraphs) checked in the background with its findings growing, measured at 4.6 s for the whole document with no call from the editor over 8 ms (engine); the AI check sending exactly its disclosure, placing problems on their quotes, dropping the missing one and withholding a replacement that loses a command (engine, fake model); the underline and a replacement taken from the context menu (kittest). Not yet: Harper's own suggestions for a span across a command are not offered (only its message), the AI's problems are not kept over a restart, and grammar for languages other than English offline (craft-proof's own simple rules for French, Spanish and others are not wired in).
