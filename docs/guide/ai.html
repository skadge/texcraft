AI assistance Desktop only

TeXCraft can use a language model to explain build errors, rewrite a passage, write a table or a diagram, draft a reference, answer questions about your project and review a section. It is off until you connect a model, and every change it proposes arrives as a suggestion for you to accept or reject. Until then, nothing of it shows in the menus or panels; and if you do not want it at all, one switch hides it everywhere.

Not yet in the browser: a web page cannot reach a model's endpoint through the suite's AI layer yet. Use the desktop app for AI assistance.

Connecting a model

TeXCraft works with any service that offers an OpenAI-compatible API, including a server on your own computer (such as llama.cpp, Ollama or vLLM). Open Settings › AI:

  1. Endpoint: the API's base address, such as https://api.example.com/v1. It must be https://, or http:// for this computer only. TeXCraft says which: "On this computer: nothing leaves it", or "Another computer: what is sent leaves this one".
  2. Token: the key the service gave you, if it needs one (a local server usually does not). It is kept in your system's keychain, never in the preferences. Hide the token masks it while you type.
  3. List Models, then choose the Model that answers. Test Connection checks that it answers.
  4. Look at What may be sent, switch off any kind of request you do not want, then choose Connect.

Connecting agrees to send the kinds of request switched on to that address and that model, without asking again each time. Another address or another model asks again (Agree to Send). Forget removes the connection, its token and the agreements.

What may be sent

Settings › AI lists each kind of request, what it sends and where, with a switch. A kind switched off is never sent: its menu entries and buttons are disabled, and say where to switch it back on.

The kinds of request and what each sends
KindUsed byWhat is sent
ExplainExplain on a build messageThe build message, its help and the source lines around it (and the preamble when the message is in it).
RewriteRewrite with AIThe selected text and a few lines around it.
Change as askedAsk AI to Change This…Your instruction, the selected text and a few lines around it.
Next wordsSuggesting the next words as you typeThe text before the caret in the current paragraph, after each pause in typing.
Make LaTeXInsert › Make with AI…Your description, the main document's preamble and, for a figure, the names of the project's images.
Draft a referenceInsert › Add a Reference with AI…The title or DOI you type, and nothing of the project.
AskThe Ask AI panelYour question and the open file, or every text file of the project when you choose it.
ReviewEdit › Review › Review This Section with AIThe section at the caret.

Each kind's instructions to the model are a prompt you can change, under Prompts in Settings › AI. Its placeholders (such as {passage} or {instruction}) are explained beside it, and Reset to Default brings back TeXCraft's own. A prompt that lost a placeholder it needs is kept but not used: TeXCraft sends its own and says so. Whatever the prompt says, the answer is checked in the same way.

Show What Was Sent lists this session's last requests: when, how many bytes, to which address and model, and how each ended, with the text that went.

Turning AI features off

Untick Use AI features, at the top of Settings › AI, and every AI feature is hidden: the AI entries of the menus (and of the macOS menu bar), of the text's context menu and of All Commands, Explain on build messages and in the problem popup, the Ask AI section of the rail, the next words as you type, and the tip of the day about AI. Nothing is sent, and the endpoint is not contacted. Settings › AI then shows only the switch, so you can turn the features back on: the connection, what you agreed to and your prompts are kept for then. The choice is kept in your preferences, and it overrides each project's own switch. Agents are refused too, saying AI is turned off in Settings; they can turn the features off, never on.

The features are on by default, but they show only once a model is connected: before that, Settings › AI is the only place they appear.

Turning AI off for a project

Untick Use AI in this project in Settings › AI: the project's sources are then never sent, whatever the other settings. The choice is kept in the project's .texcraft folder, so it travels with the project.

What it can do

Explain a build message

Explain on a card of the build log, or in the message's popup in the editor, asks the model what the message means and how to fix it. The explanation appears in the log, marked "Explained by AI" with the model's name; a proposed fix arrives as a suggested change.

Rewrite a selection

Select a passage, then Edit › Rewrite with AI (also in the text's right-click menu): Clearer, Shorter, More Formal, Fix Grammar and Spelling, or Translate to… a language. The rewrite arrives as a suggestion on the selection. A rewrite that drops or alters a label, a reference, a citation, a formula or a command is refused, saying why, so your LaTeX stays intact.

Ask AI to Change This…, in the same submenu, takes an instruction in your own words, such as "flesh out these bullet points". The change may add text and markup, but is refused if it drops or alters a label, reference, citation or formula. Your recent instructions are offered again.

The next words as you type

With Suggest the next words as I type ticked in Settings › AI (off by default), the model's guess at the next words appears in grey after the caret when you pause. Tab takes them; Escape dismisses them. This works in Vim's Insert mode too.

Make LaTeX from a description

Insert › Make with AI… writes a Table, an Equation, a TikZ Diagram or a Figure for one of the project's images, from your description. The result is typeset with your preamble and shown in the dialog before anything changes. Insert puts it at the caret, selected, as one edit that Undo takes out, with any package it needs added before \begin{document}. When it does not typeset, the dialog shows the engine's messages, and Ask Again with the Error sends them back to the model.

Draft a bibliography entry

Insert › Add a Reference with AI… drafts a BibTeX entry from a work's title or DOI. The entry arrives as a suggested change in a .bib file of your choice, under a line saying it was drafted by AI and must be checked. Models invent references: check every field before you accept it.

Ask about the project

The Ask AI section of the side panel (in the rail) answers questions about This file or The whole project. Places in the answer, such as chapters/intro.tex:42, are links to the line. Ctrl+Enter in the question sends it.

Review a section

Edit › Review › Review This Section with AI sends the section at the caret. Each remark arrives as a comment by "AI" with the model's name, on the words it quotes. Remarks that quote words not in the text are dropped, and the status line says how many.

One request runs at a time; Cancel the AI Request stops it.

Not yet: answers appear when they are complete, not word by word; and the next words are not offered in the formatted view.

Privacy in short

  • Nothing is sent until you connect a model, and then only the kinds of request you left switched on, to the address and model you agreed to.
  • With a model on your own computer, nothing leaves it.
  • The token stays in your system's keychain. The preferences keep the connection and what you agreed to, never any of your text.
  • A project can turn AI off for itself, and Use AI features turns it off, and hides it, everywhere.
  • Agents driving TeXCraft can switch kinds of request off, but never on, and cannot agree for you.
  • What the service does with what it receives is up to the service: read its terms, or use a local model.
